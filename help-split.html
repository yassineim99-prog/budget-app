How splitting works

How Nisba shares a joint bill between two people. The example in every section uses the same couple and the same €1,000 rent.

The example

Alex earns €2,600 a month and Sara earns €1,900. Together that's €4,500. Their rent is €1,000.

50/50

Each person pays the same share.

 PaysLeftAlex€500€2,100Sara€500€1,400

Simple, but the person who earns less feels it more.

By income

Each person pays the same percentage of the bill as their share of the household income. Alex earns 58% of the €4,500, so Alex pays 58% of the rent.

 PaysLeftAlex€577.78€2,022.22Sara€422.22€1,477.78

Fair when incomes differ: both give up the same part of what they earn.

Same left over

The person with more money left pays more, so that both end the month with the same amount left. Nisba looks at each person's income and their other expenses that month.

 PaysLeftAlex€850€1,750Sara€150€1,750

If the gap between you is bigger than the bill, the person with more pays the whole bill, and the other pays nothing.

Custom

Type a ratio, such as 2 and 1, or 60 and 40. With 2 and 1, Alex pays €666.67 and Sara pays €333.33.

Who paid, and settling up

Each expense also records who paid it at the till. If Alex pays the whole €1,000 rent but the split says Alex's share is €577.78, then Sara owes Alex €422.22.

The Joint tab adds this up for every joint bill in the month and shows one line, such as “Sara owes you €422.22”. When the money has been sent, tap the button under Settle up to mark it as paid.

You can mix methods: rent by income, groceries 50/50, internet same left over. The Joint tab works out the totals for you.

Tips

  • By income and Same left over need everyone's income for that month. Add each salary once and it repeats every month.
  • My share on the Home card counts only your part of each joint bill. Everyone counts the full amounts.
  • To change the split of a bill, tap it and choose another method. Monthly bills change for every month.
